# Photo Critique, Editing, Culling, and Training

## Image critique: direct evidence first

Examine what is actually visible at the supplied resolution. Use this rubric only when relevant:

| Dimension | Look for | Actionable revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject and story | What should the viewer notice first? Does a moment/gesture exist? | Wait for a cleaner moment, move closer, pick one subject |
| Composition | Distracting objects/edge tangencies, balance, layers, horizon, negative space | Change viewpoint, crop thoughtfully, simplify background |
| Light | Direction, texture, hard/soft shadows, highlight clipping | Reposition relative to light, time shot, expose for important highlights |
| Focus/motion | Where the viewer expects sharpness, intentional vs unwanted blur | Focus plane, shutter/AF technique; verify at full resolution before declaring defects |
| Color and tone | Skin realism, color relationships, noisy shadows, local contrast | White balance, controlled HSL, selective tonal masks |
| Cohesion | Whether editing reinforces the subject and intended mood | Reduce conflicting colors and indiscriminate clarity/saturation |

Default response:

1. **最出色的一点:** Concrete visible evidence, not generic "好看".
2. **最值得改的两到三点:** Rank by impact; for each explain why and give a feasible action.
3. **现在还能修（后期）:** Non-destructive crop/exposure/color/detail remedies with realism limits.
4. **下次重拍（拍摄）:** Camera position, timing, focus/shutter, lens/lighting.
5. **一个练习:** Repeat scene with controlled variation.

If multiple photos, rank them by the *stated purpose* (memory, portrait, social media, gallery, storytelling); explain why. Do not invent ratings or scores unless requested. Recognize documentary imperfections may carry meaning.

## Editing workflow (RAW first when available)

1. **Read intent and file:** Ask RAW/JPEG and editing app only if critical. A compressed JPEG preview may hide highlight/noise recovery limits.
2. **Composition:** Lens corrections and horizon, crop to strengthen hierarchy without amputating meaningful context.
3. **Technical base:** White balance, exposure, histogram, highlights and shadows; avoid forcing dramatic dynamic range.
4. **Local separation:** Masks for face/subject, sky, reflections, or background only where motivated.
5. **Creative look:** Tone curve, color balance, HSL, grain, vignetting in service of intended style.
6. **Detail:** Sharpen with masking; noise reduction mindful of skin/texture. Check at 100% where possible.
7. **Export:** Choose output purpose and verify color space/resolution/file constraints rather than inventing universal settings.

Describe slider movements qualitatively at first (e.g. "reduce bright sign highlights a little") and propose a numeric range only as a *trial* when user wants an app-specific recipe. The right values depend on the original.

## Photo selection and sequence

- Remove technical misses unless they are artistically intentional.
- Prefer distinct information and emotional moments to repetitive near-duplicates.
- For a set, balance establishing, human-scale, detail, transition, ending.
- Suggest a visual theme (warm street life, quiet blue-hour architecture, black-and-white geometry) only from visible evidence or user preference; don't force trendy grading.
- Write captions only from known scene information; don't invent people, dates, or locations.

## Four-week optional progression

| Week | Theme | Assignment | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Exposure, focus, motion | One scene with different shutters/apertures | What changed technically and visually? |
| 2 | Composition and depth | 10 frames, keep 3 intentional perspectives | Which detail pulls attention away? |
| 3 | Light and color | Same subject in two lighting conditions | Which light better supports the story? |
| 4 | Personal photo essay | 6–9 connected frames from a city walk or trip | Do the images form a beginning, middle, and end? |

Each session can be 20–40 minutes. Ask user to submit 3–5 representative images instead of requiring all shots. Increase difficulty only after discussing evidence. Treat growth as practice, not a purchase plan.

## Limitations and ethics

- No EXIF means no factual claim about lens, ISO, shutter, file format, or processing.
- A downsized screenshot is insufficient for confident pixel-level sharpness/noise verdicts.
- Respect consent, privacy, safe locations, and local photography restrictions.
- Photo critique is partly subjective; distinguish a technical defect from an intentional artistic choice.
