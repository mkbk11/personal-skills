# Field Playbooks for Nikon & General Photography

These settings are **starting estimates** for handheld photography and must be adapted by metering, light, subject speed, focal length, and keeper rate. Use the **actual confirmed kit**, not a presumed purchase. Prefer exposure mode A with minimum shutter/Auto ISO when practical, or M + Auto ISO when action shutter must be fixed. Explain camera-specific setup without inventing menu labels.

## Fast starting-point table

| Subject | Lens / range | Aperture | Shutter target | Focus / drive | First adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Daytime urban street | 24–200 at 24–50mm | f/5.6–8 | around 1/250 s for pedestrians | AF-C for motion; AF-S for static | Shorten shutter if subject movement blurs |
| Environmental portrait | 50mm prime or zoom at 50–85mm | f/1.8–4 | 1/250 s or faster for people | Eye/face-detection if supported and reliable | Stop down when both eyes/people need depth |
| Landscape / architecture | Zoom at 24–50mm | f/5.6–11 | Handheld speed according to focal length and stabilization | Single focus point or deliberate focus | Tripod for long exposure; watch highlight clipping |
| Night street / ambient portrait | 50mm prime | f/1.8–2.8 | around 1/125–1/250 s for people | AF-C/AF-S depending on movement | Raise Auto ISO before letting moving people blur |
| Night stationary architecture | Zoom or 50mm | f/4–8 | As slow as safe handheld, or tripod-supported | AF-S or manual as scene allows | Check sharpness and clipping, bracket if needed |
| Food / still-life detail | 50mm or zoom at 50–100mm | f/2.8–5.6 | around 1/125 s handheld | Select precise plane of focus | Move light or subject before excessive editing |
| Daylight sports / basketball / football | Zoom at 70–200mm (reach) or 50mm (light) | Widest useful aperture | around 1/800–1/1600 s for action | AF-C, tracking/burst if reliable | Increase ISO as necessary; anticipate movement |

Do not present this table as manufacturer recommendations or a universal preset. Some lenses cannot achieve the listed aperture at every focal length; the 24–200mm's maximum aperture varies across the zoom.

## Decision tree

1. **Moving subject?** Set a shutter that freezes *its* speed, then adjust aperture/ISO. VR/IBIS does not freeze subject motion.
2. **Subject static?** Optimize depth of field and low ISO; stabilization/tripod may permit slower shutter if handling allows.
3. **Background distracting?** Change photographer-to-subject and subject-to-background distance, angle, focal length, aperture, or time before seeking a new lens.
4. **Highlights important?** Review histogram/blinkies where available; consider exposure compensation or bracketing, keep RAW if editing.
5. **Mixed or neon lighting?** Prioritize believable skin/subject color, use RAW and correct white balance later if necessary.

## Frame variety: a short shot list

For street/travel, request three to five frames:

- **Establishing:** Wide scene showing place, weather, architecture, scale.
- **Human story:** A subject interacting with their environment, respectfully and with consent where required.
- **Detail:** Texture, sign, hands, food, reflection, or color contrast.
- **Layering:** Foreground–subject–background separation or framed subject.
- **Counterpoint:** A different focal length or angle telling a contrasting story.

For portraits: full/environmental, medium expression, detail/gesture, side or backlight variation. For landscapes: wide, tele detail, foreground perspective, changing light.

## On-location checklist

- Before: battery/cards, clean lens, correct date/time, RAW/JPEG, exposure mode, stabilization/AF check, weather and safety.
- During: identify *one subject*, clean edges, check background, look at light direction, focus the intended plane, review clipping and motion blur.
- After: mark strongest frame, note what failed and why, select one deliberate reshoot or edit.

## Learning drills (15–30 minutes)

1. **One focal length:** Pick one focal length on the 24–200mm (e.g. 35mm) and keep it fixed for five different narratives.
2. **Three backgrounds:** Same subject with near, medium, far background; keep subject size consistent to understand separation.
3. **Motion ladder:** Capture a moving friend or safe public subject around 1/125, 1/500, and 1/1000 s under suitable light; compare motion blur and ISO noise.
4. **Light study:** Shoot the same place at noon, golden hour, and blue hour if practical; discuss changes in shadows and color.
5. **One-story contact sheet:** Pick 5–7 images from a walk, order as location → people → detail → ending.
